OCR engine evaluation for junk-text-layer scans

Run 2026-08-19/20 against two real books from the live library — both 32-page scanned picture books ("Book A", id-a, and "Book B", id-b) whose pdfs carry a junk legacy Internet-Archive text layer that Folio's production pipeline (ocrmypdf --skip-text) deliberately preserves. The question: does any engine or flag change produce cleanly extractable text for the text-only reading variant, and do franken_ocr or raw Tesseract earn a role?

All variants were measured with one metric so numbers are comparable: clean-token ratio — whitespace-split tokens of length ≥3 that are purely alphabetic (including accented Italian letters), as a fraction of all tokens, over pdftotext -enc UTF-8 -nopgbrk output (or the engine's native text output where no pdf is produced).

Key findings

  • The engine was never the problem. ocrmypdf is Tesseract underneath; raw Tesseract reproduces its output almost word-for-word on pages ocrmypdf fresh-OCRs. The quality differences come from (a) whether junk legacy layers are preserved (--skip-text) and (b) ocrmypdf's internal rasterization/preprocessing, which measurably hurts these illustration-heavy scans — --redo-ocr and plain --force-ocr converge on identical, worse numbers.
  • Codec check re-confirmed: every page of both books is JPXDecode (JPEG2000) + JBIG2Decode. franken_ocr has no pure-Rust decoder for either and says so in its own error strings — which also recommend exactly the rasterize-first workaround tested here.
  • franken_ocr, rasterize-first, works and wins the metric (4.17 GB model, int8 CPU kernels, 44/44 selftest) but is operationally unattractive as primary OCR: 10–25× slower than Tesseract per book on this 8-core CPU-only host, 7.5 GB resident, and an unpredictable tail — a near-blank library-stamp page hit franken's internal 10-minute forward budget after 24 minutes wall-clock and yielded nothing (clean error, no crash). Its win is noise-suppression, not word accuracy: it introduced consistent new misreads on real prose.
  • No Markdown/EPUB niche on this content: across all 64 pages franken_ocr emitted zero headings/lists/tables — flat prose only. Its hypothesized "nice structure" secondary-pass role doesn't apply to illustrated children's prose; untested (and more plausible) for textbook-style scans.
  • Illustration/title pages are noise under every engine — they are pictures. Aggregate metrics under-weight the real user-visible win: on junk-layer prose pages (e.g. id-b p.7), raw Tesseract and franken_ocr both recover correct readable Italian where the production companion has pure garbage.
  • Why franken_ocr misreads Italian (2026-08-21 follow-up): its underlying model, Baidu Unlimited-OCR (arXiv:2606.23050), is DeepSeek-OCR with swapped decoder attention, continued-trained just 4,000 steps on a language-undocumented corpus — so its language knowledge is DeepSeek-OCR's, whose paper (arXiv:2510.18234) states ~25M of its 30M training pages are Chinese+English, with high-quality annotations for CN/EN only; Italian sits in a coarse ~5M-page tail shared across ~98 languages. No language list, no --lang option anywhere in franken_ocr, no per-language benchmarks beyond EN/CN columns. The architecture compresses each page to ~100 vision tokens and leans on the decoder's language prior to reconstruct text — with a thin Italian prior it emits non-words ("rigotto", "Segnite") and drops accents exactly where dictionary-tuned Tesseract ita reads the same rasters correctly. An Italian continued fine-tune is genuinely feasible (LoRA decoder-only, proven five times by the community on this model — never yet for Italian): see the dedicated research note Un DeepSeek-OCR che capisca l'italiano.

Standing verdict

Keep ocrmypdf --skip-text as the primary pipeline — third eval to reach this conclusion (2026-08-15, 2026-08-19, 2026-08-20). Do not adopt --redo-ocr/--force-ocr (measurably worse + 26–34× size without jbig2/pngquant) or franken_ocr as primary (speed/RAM/tail-latency; new word errors).

The one actionable upgrade identified: a per-book, opt-in "deep re-OCR the text version" path that rasterizes at 300 dpi and runs raw Tesseract (PSM 3, -l ita) to feed the text companion only — the text-only variant needs exactly flat text, sidestepping both ocrmypdf's preprocessing degradation and the pdf-size problem, at ~5 predictable minutes per book on the :conversion queue.
